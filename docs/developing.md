# Developing Barkstreet Snakes

For people working on the game: how to run it locally, the checks and experiments, and how it is put together. The game itself is described for players in the [README](../README.md).

## Switches for the checks

- `--root DIR` runs the simulation on another copy of the game files.
- The browser suites (scope, render, input, layout, ui) drive a headless Chrome through `tools/browser.js`: it looks in
  the usual places, or set `CHROME=/path/to/chrome`; `CI=1` runs it without its sandbox, which is needed as root
  (`scripts/test.sh` sets it).
- Optional: `SIM_WORKERS` sets the simulation's cores, `BARKBITE_PRIORITY=normal` for timing runs, `ONLINE_SEEN=1`
  makes online-test print what it saw, `LAYOUT_JOB=N` runs one of the layout suite's five CI jobs.
- To play it locally, `.claude/launch.json` serves the folder on port 5173.

## PR pictures

On the orphan branch `pr-media`, never merged, named by PR number (`pr41-*.jpg`): start from `git ls-tree
origin/pr-media`, add each file with `git hash-object -w`, build the new tree with `git mktree`, commit it with
`git commit-tree <tree> -p origin/pr-media`, and push that commit to `refs/heads/pr-media` (never force). Link pictures
by commit SHA (`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HaakTuah/barkstreet-snakes/<sha>/<name>`), never by a branch that may
be deleted. `tools/dev/crop.js` and `tools/dev/montage.js` make them.

## Run locally

Serve the folder with any static server (for example `npx serve` or `python -m http.server`) and open it in a browser. Opening `index.html` directly works too.

- `?perf` shows a frame-time overlay (toggle it with the ` key).
- `?debug` adds `SnakeDebug` to the console, for example `SnakeDebug.setup(['sniper', 'mason', 'ghost'])` to start a round against chosen rivals, or to watch them: **Watch AIs** from its card watches them, with a fourth at random.
- `node tools/records-test.js` checks that shared and played-together rounds land in each friend's Hall of Fame exactly once, and that a browser that won't keep records still has them until the tab closes.
- `node tools/online-test.js` plays rounds through the online protocol and checks that a friend's screen matches the host's every tick, and that the plans a friend draws ahead from (where everyone goes next) are the host's; it stages chokes and nudges too, so every round crosses some, and every way to be out (all ten, read from engine.js) must cross at least once, staged if the rounds don't bring it. The same goes for every kind of event the engine makes: digs and dug dives, knocks (a bead's colour, and the bird eating one) and where the strike promised each would land, beetles digging and underground, the burrows as the engine keeps them (opened, last used, dug), one a dig opened and one closing over; and it feeds the friend's screen malformed ones of each (a burrow off the board or not opened on a tick among them).
- `node tools/simulate.js 200` plays 200 AI-only rounds headlessly with the game's own rules and AI, and prints each personality's win rate, food, kills, how snakes die and so on. Add `--seed 1` to repeat a run exactly (on any machine), `--check` to fail on a stalled round, `--census` to count what a round holds (golden things, weather, lightning, the woodpecker, powers, how hungry snakes are before the woodpecker comes and after, and meals a snake-minute mid-round; the beetles: how each one above ground ends, caught, down a burrow, dug in, flown off or pecked, the same for the chased ones and how long a catch takes, digs and those caught digging, dives into burrows a dig left, burrows open and cells free for an apple; and the woodpecker's fairness: food knocked aside, how much the bird ate and what a snake ate where it landed, and how soon, marks that shut a snake in and the deaths after them, crumbs of log cut off, and how often the biggest island's snake wins against a blind pick), `--signatures` to measure each personality's trademark against the field, and `--root <dir>` to play another copy of the game, to compare versions.
- `node tools/bench-ai.js [--base REV|DIR] [--root DIR] [--seed N] [--repeat R] [--only before|after]` times the rivals' thinking: `decide()` on positions recorded from seeded AI-only rounds, in two copies of the game side by side (by default the last commit's and the files as they are), and checks that every position decides the same in both. Run it before the `simulate.js` fingerprints whenever `ai.js` changes: it's quicker, and it says which positions went another way. Its CPU time is the deciding thread's alone, leaving out the compiling V8 does in the background (a whole `simulate.js` run pays for that).
- `node tools/rules-test.js [--seed N]` (N: other randomness for the checks that don't seed their own; the same run every time without it) checks the rules on hand-built boards: tails, head-ons, starving, the woodpecker's manners (shutting nobody in while it has another patch, taking the crumbs, rounder hollows, and where knocked food lands: each tie, never onto a body, food, a hole, fire or a burrow, the strike's own list and the bird eating what has nowhere to go; the ring a promise, made 5 moves before the strike and worked out on the log as it'll be then: a ring holds as a tail passes over it, food beside a snake the strike pecks lands on its ring, a ring a head moves onto goes by the rule, never onto a ring kept for another piece), beetles (two at once and never three, in sudden death too, digging in and caught mid-dig, a dig a head coming straight from outside its reach gets to on its last tick, the burrow a dig leaves and the one unused longest closing over), beads, and food a cough rolls aside (into a turn, so a staircase can't dribble it along; rolling on over food when both sides are taken, a beetle facing the way it ran), never on a burrow, what the AIs can see, the rivals' reading of the woodpecker and the beetles (knocked food valued where it'll land and never waited for in the mark, left to a rival nearer it, raced for by Gobbler and a desperate rival; the burrows as they are, so a beetle bound for one isn't chased and one with none to run for digs in and is taken; a digging beetle taken in time or left alone, before and in sudden death; a second beetle read like the first; and in played rounds, Gobbler taking an apple where the strike knocked it, and catching a cornered beetle as it digs in), and Star's habit (it flashes and jumps where nobody else would, round a body ahead rather than into it, and goes for a golden leaf over a nearer apple), and the owner's playtest: every rival takes food on the edge, a golden apple in a corner, and an apple beside holes, fire or another snake when nothing threatens it, and still refuses one where it would be pinned or cut off.
- `node tools/chatter-test.js` checks that every line the rivals say has its Chinese, for Copycat, that no Chinese is left over from a line that's gone, and that every line marked as a punchline is one someone says; then it plays the chatter with the game's own engine through staged moments (pecked under, put out by the rain, food burning or rotting nearby, starving, cut by a golden flash, flashing, the last apples, and Star showing off at most every 10 seconds, even at its most addicted; choking, with every voice's line and its rest, and the thanks for food a cough knocked aside; a colony or capital the woodpecker took; a beetle that dug in and got away, with its hunter's line and rest, from a hunter as far off as `BEETLE_DIG_FEAR` and no further; food the woodpecker knocked aside and someone else ate, and the grumble, from the nearest head to where it lay, other than the eater's, within 8 cells) and a few whole AI rounds, which count the chokes, the digs and the knocks and the lines for them, and check every grumble against that rule (`--rounds N` plays N more random rounds for it). The names in the log are in their snakes' own colours, readable on its wood (Copycat the flag's red, not pink). Last, it runs the sound (audio/ on a stand-in AudioContext) and checks that a rival's flash, jump, golden flash, catching fire, golden mushroom, dig or knock never silences yours, that your own golden flashes or golden jumps in a row are each heard, that each choke, cough, dig and knock is heard, that a rival's dig keeps its legs' scratches past its dulling and a burrow closing over has flecks of sawdust high enough to carry over the music, that two burrows closing over at once are one crumble, and that mute silences them.
- `node tools/input-test.js` checks the controls in headless Chrome with real key and touch events on a steady 60 Hz clock: Space or a tap jumps, E, Shift or a swipe ahead flashes, nothing else fires a power, a swipe never jumps, and a press reaches the next tick. A thumb resting before it swipes or while the other swipes, and a quick double flick, never fire a power; a tap that drifts as it lifts still jumps, a long slanted stroke still turns, and mashing jump over your death's card never starts a round. The mouse gets real presses too: the left button jumps and the right flashes, both together go in turn, no menu pops up over the board, clicks on a card or off the board fire nothing, and mashing the mouse over a death card never plays again or focuses the carve field. It also checks the lessons and their sounds, and that every lesson is 60 characters or fewer, with keys and on touch: the choke and the nudge; a beetle digging in near enough to catch (heard as yours, or as a rival's when another head cornered it; a cell further off, it teaches nothing); the woodpecker's lesson, once its own banner has had its time, and its knock (once a strike, from the first piece that flies, nothing for food the bird ate); a beetle down a burrow or up out of one scrabbling; a dug dive and the burrow it leaves silent (no creak, no news of a burrow cracking open), that burrow's lesson the first time the banner's free for one and once a round (a far dig's burrow teaching the dig and the burrow at once, and neither again), a burrow closing over teaching its rule once the burrow's is told, a burrow cracking open creaking and one closing over crumbling, and a flag or a capital the woodpecker took teaching its rule (a colony let go says nothing). And the banners: each held for its reading time, the next a breather after the last, round news waiting behind a waiting lesson rather than cutting in and losing it, a lesson whose moment has passed let go, at the board's top or foot and never its middle, and on a phone held upright every banner of the game's one line over the title row, its words whole, at 360 and 320 px. And `SnakeDebug.setup`'s rivals are the ones **Watch AIs** watches.
- `node tools/ui-test.js` checks the owner's playtest in headless Chrome, with a mouse, keys and touch on a desktop and two phones: a rival's note on the napping card, your death's card and the round's stays until you close it (and how), keeps up with the round, says nothing while you play and is a banner, up as long as it takes to read, while you watch; and the Field guide's doors (its button, G, How to play, the nap card's link, your death's card opening it at what got you), each opening the book on its own tab, at what got you even when it's far down (a death by fire), its close on the screen; the round's card when your death ends it naming what got you too; the header's rows on phones, over the board (the guide named on half a 1080p screen's window and on an iPad mini and an iPad held upright, every button on a row with another in a narrow window) and beside it (a 1080p desktop's browser: the doors' own row, both named), and at every step of it, with six buttons and five, a mouse and touch, nothing moving there as Score and Best grow to 100 and 1000; a laptop's column and a 1080p browser's that don't scroll the page with the chatter full, and a narrow one's six icons in a row; and a real first good round's cards fitting phones' boards, upright and on their sides, with the longest words: the start card, your death's (a note on it or not, its Field guide link back once you've carved your name on the smallest) and the round's when your death ends it.
- `node tools/render-test.js` checks the renderer's promises a picture can show, in headless Chrome: a flash drawn ahead and gliding on, a corpse's beads in its markings, power pips on the board at its edge, scorch as soot, every Field guide picture, Star's look (a star for a head, its rainbow flowing back from it, its tell, its beads, its mustache gag), jumps (the arch, the shadow across the hurdle, dust and trail, and none of the motion with reduced motion), bridges (shade along the bands too, sag, the shadow in the pit on its lit floor, a drooping tail), choking (the bead held in the mouth through the cheek puff and spat from the cheek, its arc, a shut mouth and cough puffs big enough for a phone, the head's lurch, rolls and scurries), a pecked flag (it tips and falls, the land shrinks into the pit, a banner carried), the strike's land drawn ahead (pixel for pixel a rebuild), Star's rainbow streaming (back as it goes, still when stunned, a burst on a flash, a slow drift with reduced motion), two beetles, one digging in, the burrow it leaves freshly bored and settling into an ordinary one (no heap without a burrow), a burrow closing over (sawdust and moss, gone in about a second; ### Running experiments

A balance question ("does this shorten sudden death?", "did my change hurt anyone?") is cheapest answered in three steps:

1. **Screen it with `tools/abtest.js`**, which plays two copies of the game on the same seeded rounds, paired, and stops as soon as every number it waits for has a verdict: B higher, lower, or the same within a margin that matters. It plays 100 rounds a seed at a time (seeds 3 and 11, up to 600 each), so a clear answer often takes a look or two instead of 600 rounds a seed on each side, and its intervals stay honest however early it stops and however many numbers it shows: the chance that any verdict in its table is wrong is at most 5%. By default it asks about the round's pace (`length`, the median round, and `sd`, how long sudden death lasts and how many rounds reach it). Ask only for what the question is about, since every number asked for widens every interval a little, and it stops only when all of them are decided: `--metrics` picks from `length`, `sd`, `deaths`, `starving` and `powers`; `win` and `place`, each personality's win rate and average place (shown, but not waited for: a few hundred rounds each can't pin them within a margin that matters); and `trademarks` (each personality's, as `--signatures` measures them) when a change mustn't blunt them.

   `node tools/abtest.js . ../variant --metrics sd,deaths`

2. **Fast-forward a late-game question.** `--at 120000` stores every round at 2:00, as A plays it, and plays both copies on from there. The openings, most of a round's work, are played once, and the two sides are the same round up to the woodpecker, so a difference shows sooner. By hand, `simulate.js --save-at` stores the rounds (and prints where) and `--from` goes on with them under any copy; with the same files, every round comes out exactly as played straight through.

   `node tools/abtest.js . ../variant --at 120000 --metrics sd`

   `node tools/simulate.js 600 --seed 3 --save-at 120000`, then `node tools/simulate.js --from FILE --root ../variant --census`

3. **Confirm once, at the end, on fresh seeds.** Seeds 3 and 11 chose what you kept, so their rounds can't confirm it: a variant that won a lucky streak there would only be confirmed by the same streak. Ask the same question on seeds the screening never played, and take the numbers you'll report from those rounds:

   `node tools/abtest.js . ../variant --seed 29 --seed 41 --metrics sd,deaths`

   `node tools/simulate.js 600 --seed 29 --census --root ../variant` (and `--seed 41`). `--census` includes a table for each personality (flashes and jumps a minute, golden pickups, chokes, crashes survived, time alive, food, kills), so there's no need for a harness of your own.

Every seeded round is cached by the files that played it, round by round, so asking again or for more rounds plays only the new ones: the A side of one experiment is free in the next, and the rounds the confirming abtest played (without `--at`) are free in its `--census`. The files are read when a run's turn comes, and what's stored is what played: a file edited while a run waits is played as it is then. Only one simulation runs on the machine at a time (the next waits its turn), on half the cores, at low priority.
### How it's put together

| File | What it does |
|------|--------------|
| `tuning.js` | Every number that shapes how the game plays and feels, in one place. |
| `engine.js` | The rules: moving, eating, dying, hunger, the woodpecker. No drawing, so it also runs in Node. |
| `ai.js` | The rivals' brains and personalities. |
| `render/` | Draws the board on a canvas, with smooth movement between ticks: nineteen files by what they draw, sharing one scope (the list is at the top of `render/core.js`). |
| `audio/` | Sound effects and the adaptive music, synthesised live: `cues.js` (every cue), then `sound.js` (the synthesiser, playing, music, weather; makes `SnakeAudio`). |
| `chatter/` | The rivals' speech bubbles and chat log: their lines, in English and Chinese, and the talk (the list is at the top of `chatter/voices.js`). |
| `records.js` | The Hall of Fame and your stats. |
| `online.js` | Play with friends: the messages between host and friends. |
| `net.js` | Play with friends: the connections (PeerJS, loaded only when used). |
| `game/` | Ties it together: the game loop, input, the cards, the controls and the lobby: seven files by what they do (the list is at the top of `game/core.js`). |
