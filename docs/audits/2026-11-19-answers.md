### Q1. Can what each system offers be said in a sentence, and do the others use only that?
**Scale: 2**

What each system offers, and its entrance (uses.json `namespaces`; members are those other parts use):
- `tuning.js`: every number of play and feel (`TUNING`).
- `engine/`: the rules, `createGame`, `step`, `aiView` and more (`SnakeEngine`, 21 members).
- `ai/`: a rival's move from what it sees (`SnakeAI`: `decide`, `PERSONAS`).
- `chatter/`: what the rivals say at moments (`SnakeChatter`, 9).
- `audio/`: cues, music and weather sound (`SnakeAudio`, 10).
- `render/`: a `Game` drawn on the canvas (`SnakeRender`, 21).
- `records.js` and `lineup.js`: saved results (`SnakeRecords`, 26).
- `net.js` and `net-relays.js`: a room over PeerJS and relays (`SnakeNet`, 3).
- `online.js`: a round's ticks encoded and applied (`SnakeOnline`, 13).

`game/` has no such sentence. It holds the page's elements, the narrator's words (`FLAVOR`, game/core.js:68), the state, the loop, input, cards, HUD, banners, the Hall of Fame and Field guide, and the online lobby: 9 files, 320 shared names, a median of 14 files reached (uses.json `perPartFileReach`). It is also the only part that uses 9 namespaces (`namespaceMatrix.game`).

Between parts, the namespaces carry nearly all of it: 1,679 mentions stay inside a part and 75 cross (`mentionsWithinAPartAndAcross`). These go around the entrances (`matrixNames`):
- **Engine to AI:** engine/core.js:40 and :89 to :115 use `DIRS` (ai/core.js:54), `PERSONAS` (ai/rivals.js:87) and `POOL` (ai/moods.js:5). engine/core.js:37 also reads `window.SnakeAI.decide` as it loads.
- **AI to engine, at run time:** ai/food.js:165 to :185 calls `SnakeEngine.knocks` and `beakDepth`. Together with the line above, **the two depend on each other in a circle that no measure shows**: uses.json counted ai→SnakeEngine as "0 members" because the alias is a `let` assigned later (`engine = window.SnakeEngine || {}`); the reader found it by hand and uses.js reads it since.
- **Game to AI:** 8 game files read the tuning through the AI's alias `TUNE` (ai/core.js:62). game/ declares no alias of its own.
- **Render to engine:** 31 render files use the bare `T` and `GRID`. render/cut.js:62 and :71 call `newIsles` and `actOf` as bare globals rather than through `SnakeEngine`.
- **Game to render:** game/banners.js:230 asks render/jumps.js:137 `inRingReach` a question about the rules.
- **Root to game:** update.js:47 and :121 read `recordsEl`, `pickerEl`, `state` and `online`, and call `openFriends` and `hostRoom`.

Inside a part, sharing is free: render has 952 names over 34 files, and render/world.js reaches 27 files and 107 names (`widestFiles`).

Why 2: `game/` is the shared middle that most work passes through (Q3, Q4).

**The part check (for A's spread, D, E and I), and whether the core's shape check applies.**

The core's `scripts/shape.mjs` reads systems, entrances, crossings and cycles from **imports**. Here there are none, so it would see no crossing at all. Its rule for classic scripts, "each file adds at most one name to the shared global space", would list 128 of 129 scripts (1,734 names) on its first run. That is a baseline nobody can pay down with small PRs short of wrapping every file, so I would not adopt that rule here. Its **function marks** (lines, depth, branching) do apply unchanged, and they hold B.

The cheaper check for this game is to promote `tools/dev/audit/uses.js` into a suite in scripts/test.sh. Its config names each part, the namespace that is its entrance, and the parts it may use. It fails:
- a new bare name crossing parts that is not in its baseline (today's 13 names: uses.json `matrixNames`);
- a namespace used by a part that doesn't declare it;
- any shared name or namespace of the display used by engine or ai (`rulesReachingIntoTheDisplay`, empty today);
- a namespace alias it cannot read, such as ai/food.js:166, instead of counting 0.

It reads only text and runs in about the time of the `unused` suite (a guess; not measured).

### A split or move to imports: what it would gain and where to begin

**What it would gain here:**
- The core's shape check would then read entrances, crossings and cycles for free.
- Each file would state what it uses, so a builder reads less.
- The tools' order-based loading would go: `game-files.js` filtering index.html, `parts.js`, the `vm` contexts.
- Imported bindings are read-only. That would force C, D and H into the open: no cross-file write to `ctx`, and the 218 `ai/` `let`s would have to become a decision object.

**What it would cost here, honestly:**
- **"Opening `index.html` directly works too"** (docs/developing.md:74) ends. Browsers refuse module scripts over `file://`.
- Module scripts are deferred and strict, so start-up order changes; this needs measuring with the coming start-up timings.
- About 130 module requests form a chain of imports instead of a flat list. They would need `modulepreload` lines, one more list to keep beside sw.js.
- **The measuring tools rest on loading game texts into fresh contexts:**
  - the sim's seeded `Math` and its workers (tools/sim/00-setup.js:41 to :60);
  - `--versus`, two AIs in one context (`gameFiles.apart`);
  - abtest loading a copy at another git revision (`gameFiles(null, read)`).
  - Node caches ES modules and has no stable `vm` module loader, so each of these needs re-engineering, and their fingerprints must stay identical.
