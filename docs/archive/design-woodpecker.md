# The woodpecker, as it was decided: the round files' text

The woodpecker's sections as the round files and the owner's playtest had them, word for word, with their superseded
versions and measurements.
The current rules are in `docs/design/38-the-woodpecker.md`.

## From the round's arc (`12-the-rounds-arc.md`)

**The woodpecker comes at 2:00, not 3:00** (your call, and the numbers agree). With hunger, food is what keeps a round going, and late rounds were dragging. Over 600 simulated AI rounds the median round lasts 71 s, the middle half 46-112 s, and 21% of rounds reach the woodpecker, so it's a real event rather than a rarity. Every personality wins 15-31% of the rounds it plays. (Gobbler had slipped to 8%: it chased food straight into other snakes. It now flinches from heads a little more, so it lives longer and, true to form, eats a third more.)

**The woodpecker, rebuilt: big, telegraphed, in chunks** (since reworked again: patches anywhere, see "Crashes, not deaths"). It used to sit on the board as a small sprite, pecking a cell every other tick. Now a big great spotted woodpecker (about six cells tall) lands beside the board, on its own layer over the page, on whichever side has room on this screen (with room nowhere, as on a phone, shrunk into the gutter on the side nearer its patch, its beak never over a cell). Then, over and over: it picks the next run of up to 8 free cells along the outermost ring left (never within 3 cells of a head, never a body or food), eyes it for 13 ticks (about a second and a half; the chunk glows red and cracks, the pulse quickening, and the bird crouches), swoops in, drums it all out in a shower of chips (except any cell a body is on by then), flies back, and rests for 6 ticks. The AIs keep out of a marked chunk as they keep out of holes, with their usual reaction delay. Over 300 simulated rounds the median round is as long as before (69 s); deaths in its holes fell from 0.29 to 0.06 per woodpecker round, because every strike is announced, so the squeeze now comes from shrinking room and hunger (starving rose from 2% to 5% of deaths). Online it's protocol 3.

## From crashes, not deaths (`14-crashes-not-deaths-the-rules-reworked.md`)

**The woodpecker, gentler and stranger.** It no longer eats the log from the edges in: every `PECK_REST_TICKS` (36, about 3.5 s) it picks a patch of `PECK_CHUNK` (7) cells grown at random from a start anywhere on the log, aimed clear of every body and all food and more than `PECK_CLEARANCE` (2) cells from every head while there's such a spot (failing that, clear of bodies; failing that, anywhere). It eyes it for `PECK_AIM_TICKS` (17, about 1.6 s; the AIs keep out, like a hole), swoops in and drums it out. **Whatever's in the patch when it strikes goes**: food, fire, a burrow, and any part of a snake (*since round six A*, food in it is knocked aside instead: see "The woodpecker plays fair"). A body caught there is cut, and everything behind the cut falls off as beads; a head caught there (or a cut that leaves less than 3) is out. Sudden death still ends rounds: no new food, apples rotting away, and hunger. *Superseded by round four* (see "The woodpecker: holes under you"): patches of 9 growing to 16, aimed clear of food and more than 4 cells from every head, a third of them starting under a body; a body across the patch bridges the new hole instead of being cut, only a head on the ground falls in, and the last apples fall one at a time.

## From round four (`24-round-four-the-owners-feedback-and-the-calls.md`)

### The woodpecker: holes under you, and holes that look like holes

- **It pecks under bodies too.** A third of its strikes start under a snake (`PECK_UNDER_BODY`, any body but never a head; the same share on every strike, the first included). A body lying over a fresh hole **bridges it**: it isn't cut and drops nothing, and the cell is an ordinary hole once the tail has passed. Only a head on the ground in the patch falls in. A jump whose landing was taken meanwhile comes down on a bridging body, not into the hole under it; if that body moves on while the head is still there, stunned, it falls in. (Round four's critique: it was left standing on an open hole, and walked off it.)
  - *Why:* the old cut took 32 segments on average and was decided by reaction time. *Measured*, 40 of 49 cuts were heads already heading into the mark, exactly within the 240 ms nobody can react in.
- **It aims at least 5 cells from every head** (`PECK_CLEARANCE` 4, from 2), and clear of all food, while there's such a spot, so nobody can be inside a fresh mark before they can see it. (In 600 AI rounds on each of two seeds it had to aim nearer a head 4 times in about 20,000 aims; 6 times on the final build.) *Since round six A* it may cover food, knocking it aside, and shuts nobody in while it has another patch to pick: see "The woodpecker plays fair".
- **Fewer, bigger hollows.** Half the strikes widen an old hollow (`PECK_WIDEN`), and patches grow compact. The log ends up with a few big hollows instead of a pegboard.
- **Bigger patches** (decided in the balance pass, once the rest was built): 9 cells, a cell bigger each strike up to 16 (`PECK_CHUNK`, `PECK_CHUNK_MAX`; from 7 and 14). See "Events and timings". (*Since round six A*, 12 growing to 20, with shorter rests.)
- **Holes stay permanent and only ever added.** The squeeze is what ends rounds, and online play relies on it.
- **The look.** One pit per connected hollow, traced along the cell edges:
  - a torn, inked lip;
  - a dark floor;
  - a far wall lit from the top left like everything else;
  - splinters over the rim and sawdust round it;
  - at the board's edge, a notch bitten out of the log.

  They lie on the static layer under the snakes, so a body bridging a hole is drawn across it, and a strike paints again only the box round its new holes. The old look, one round dark disc a cell, read as bubble wrap.
- **The aim marker.** It's traced with the same outline, its rim the pit's torn lip to come (the same wobble, corners rounder: 11 units against the pits' 7), and still glows red. Its cracks run along the grain under the snakes, and as the strike nears the pecks bite nicks in from outside the rim, each its own size and unevenly spaced, first and closest together where the bird is at it. (Evenly spaced dents on the rim, as first built, read as a dotted selection box.)
- **The rivals tell a mark from a hole** (`view.pecks`, added at the merge). A mark isn't a hole yet, so a golden jump or a jump for food may hop real holes but never a mark; a flash, plain or golden, treats a mark as a wall.
- **The first time it pecks under you:** "Pecking under you! Only your head can fall in." (It replaces "The woodpecker took your tail.")

### Events and timings (its woodpecker bullet)

- **Changed once the rest was built:** the woodpecker's patches start at 9 cells and grow to 16 (`PECK_CHUNK`, `PECK_CHUNK_MAX`; from 7 and 14). With a flash for everyone and rivals that see starving coming, nearly every snake now reaches 2:00, so sudden death ran a minute. Now it's about 50 s, and the median round about 170 s. (The woodpecker's speed turned out to be the weak lever: hungrier rests changed nothing, and shorter shortest rests saved 2 s.) (*Since round six A*, patches of 12 growing to 20 with shorter rests, since fewer, rounder holes take as long to fill the log.)

## From round six A (`27-round-six-a-fairness-and-fixes.md`)

**The woodpecker plays fair** (the owner saw Mute shut in by a mark beside it, with no way to plan for it, and said the bird "maybe refuses to peck where there is apples and mushrooms. Makes for some rather oddly shaped holes, with 1 tile islands."). A study played each idea alone and together, 300 seeded rounds to screen and 600 on two seeds to confirm. What's kept:
- **It won't shut you in if it can help it.** It won't pick a patch that leaves any snake less room than its length (room judged with the real timings, tails leaving), while it has another to pick. (Called "it never shuts you in" at first, and said so in the lesson and the Field guide; with nowhere else to aim it still may, 0.08 marks a round, so the verify pass softened the words.)
- **No crumbs.** A patch that would cut off an island of one or two cells takes those cells too. They're in the mark from the start, so you see them go. If a snake is on one, it picks another patch; food on one is knocked aside with the rest.
- **The log stays one piece** (2s, built 1 November 2026; it grows "No crumbs" to any size. The owner, 31 October 2026: "we want to avoid the islands stuff. it becomes too much rng, and isn't very exciting", and "No outline should be banned from falling, but ideally, it never takes out half the trunk."). The log is the biggest piece of bark once a patch is out; any other piece the patch cuts loose goes into the mark from the aim (`w.fall`), shaded dark with pale streaks sliding down it, and falls with the strike: it tips away into the pit with a creak and a far-off thud, food and beetles on it gone with it, a head still on it down with it ("Down the hole!"; a body lying over it bridges the new hole, as over any patch). The bird aims small first: no piece over `PECK_FALL_SOFT` (12 cells) while it can, then any (2s+: its first two tries, keeping clear of heads and shutting nobody in, never cut a bigger piece loose, and a rules test proves it; the tries after them take a piece of any size, so no size is banned, and a rules test proves that too). A falling piece keeps the patch's clearance from heads (`PECK_CLEARANCE`), so nobody is on it before they can see it, and the rivals, who keep out of a mark as out of a hole, never need to walk off one (they are shown which cells fall, `aiView`'s `fall`, so they never count on food there being knocked aside); a crumb of `PECK_NO_ORPHANS` (2) cells or fewer only never under a head. The last try, as for the patch ("failing that, anywhere left"), takes anything: only there, when no patch keeps clear of heads at all, can a piece cut loose have a head on it (every try before it, unfair ones included, keeps a falling piece as clear of heads as the patch). Then, as with any patch there, the mark shows it the whole aim, and a snake that gets off in time lives; one still on it at the strike falls with it ("Down the hole!"; the rules test "a head on a piece cut loose falls with it"). *The lesson:* "Cut loose, it falls! Get off the shaded piece.", the first time a mark cuts one loose (or as it falls, if the banner was busy). *Online:* protocol 20 (the mark's `fall`, the strike's `fall`). *Measured* (200 rounds each, seeds 3 and 11, before and after): rounds ending with the last snakes on islands of their own 51% and 44% to 0%; strikes leaving the log in pieces 0; pieces cut loose 1.35 and 1.59 a woodpecker round, 6.1 and 6.9 cells each, over 12 cells 0.01 and 0.03; snakes down with one 0.00 and 0.02 a round; marks shutting a snake in 0.22 to 0.08 and 0.21 to 0.12 a round, sealed deaths 6.7% to 2.3% and 6.6% to 3.3% of sudden-death deaths; round length 175 s either way, sudden death 55 to 56 s and 55 s; deaths by cause and every trademark the same within noise.
- **It knocks food aside.** Patches may cover food now. Before, it kept off food, and the holes went round apples and mushrooms in odd shapes. At the strike each piece is knocked to the nearest free cell outside the patch (free: no body, food, hole, fire or burrow; nothing grows on a burrow, and food there would stop it up). Ties go to the one nearest the middle of the log, then the first by row. It's a rule, never chance, so a rival can work it out, and so can you: through the aim the food in the mark trembles, and a faint ring shows where it will land.
  - *The ring is a promise* (the verify pass, and the critique): through the aim the rings are worked out on the log as it will be at the strike, so a ring on a body that will have moved on by then stays put as the tail passes. Five moves before the strike (`PECK_PROMISE_TICKS`, about half a second) the landings they show are kept, and each piece lands on its ring while that cell is still free at the strike. Only a head or a beetle on a ring at the strike sends that piece elsewhere: as near its ring as it can land, by the same rule. (The first build sent it by the rule from where it lay, and once in about 1,000 pieces that was 13 cells from its ring; a promise you can count on should break small. The verify pass kept the rings of the aim's last tick, worked out on the board as it stood, so they still moved as tails passed, up to the last 0.2 s.)
- **Rounder hollows.** After a patch grows, any cell beside it with three or more of its neighbours in the patch (or old holes, or the edge) is filled in, and one-cell spurs are dropped. The holes read as hollows pecked on purpose, not scribbles.
- **Bigger bites, shorter rests:** patches of 12 cells growing to 20 (were 9 to 16), and rests from 30 ticks down to 10 (were 36 to 12). Sudden death stays as long, since fewer, rounder holes take as long to fill the log.
- **It still comes at 2:00.** Arrival tied to how full the log is was tried; it moved the round's length around without making it fairer.
- **The cue:** the mark covers the crumbs it will take; knocked food trembles, and a ring shows where it'll land; at the strike, the food flies there in an arc.
- **The lesson:** once a round, as the woodpecker comes, "Food in the woodpecker's mark flies to its ring." It's the one idea you can act on, in 48 characters, one line on a phone upright or on its side; that it won't shut you in if it can help it is the Field guide's. (It was "The woodpecker knocks food aside, and won't shut you in if it can help it.", and before the verify pass "...and never shuts you in." The critique: 74 characters and two ideas, two lines on a phone in every sudden death.)
- **The rivals** see the same, and since their pass (wave 2) they use it:
  - `aiView` gives the strike's tick while the bird aims (anyone sees it drum the patch, its last blow on the strike) and, from the promise, the promised landings (the rings show them to everyone);
  - a rival works out each landing with the engine's own `E.knocks`, on the board as it sees it (the other snakes a moment late), and values the food there from the move after the strike's tick. Never where it lies: that's a hole by then, and the mark is no place to wait;
  - knocked food is anyone's, as any food is: whoever would get to the landing first counts it as theirs. It pulls from when it lands, and every move a rival would be there before then counts a little more (`EARLY`, 0.1 a move), so it waits nearby and is on the food as it lands. A rival that's nearer makes it a race, which Gobbler and Vulture run by their weights, and so does anyone desperate;
  - a beetle in the mark is knocked to its landing as the search moves it, and stops digging;
  - (a first build counted knocked food as a rival's own only if no rival at all could be there as it landed: early in the aim that was nearly every rival on the log, so the even-tempered ones seldom went for it.)

  *Measured* (600 rounds, seeds 3 / 11, against 303c4dd, the rivals as wave 1 left them): food knocked aside 5.5 / 5.8 a round; eaten by a snake where it landed, within 6 s, 2.10 / 2.06 (2.05 / 2.04), a median 17 / 18 ticks after the strike (18 / 18). Rivals always saw it the moment it landed, and it lands beside the mark, near whoever's there, so the pass changes who is waiting more than how much gets eaten.
- *Crossing the mark* (a rival leaving a marked patch before the strike) is left for PR B, with powers that let you do it on purpose.
- **As built** (wave 2):
  - *Not shutting you in:* room is a timed flood of the engine's own (tails leaving, stuns and growth counted) against the snake's length plus what it has still to grow; a snake already shut in doesn't count. It tries for a patch clear of heads that shuts nobody in, then one nearer a head that shuts nobody in, then the old three tries.
  - *Crumbs* are in the mark from the aim, in every try but the last. They may lie nearer a head than the clearance: walled in by the mark and holes, nobody can step into one.

**A woodpecker you can believe** (the owner: "Could see it flapping its wings and wobbling ... see its head tilt towards where it is aiming next ... maybe even its eyes can look around like the snakes? And maybe it properly pecks multiple times like a real woodpecker, with the sound a woodpecker makes?"). It moved like a cut-out: a glide between the perch and the patch, a crouch, a thrust. Now it moves the way a great spotted woodpecker does:
- **It flies like one:** a few quick wingbeats, then wings tucked in a dip, then beats again (the bounding flight that tells a woodpecker from other birds), its body tipping with the swoop. It lands with a flare of the wings and a little settle.
- **Perched, it's alive:** it clings upright, braced on its stiff tail as woodpeckers do, and shifts its grip now and then. It bobs a little, and its head moves the way birds' heads move: quick jerks, then still.
- **It looks:** its eye looks at things, as the snakes' eyes do. Resting, it glances at whatever moves nearby, now and then at you, and blinks. Through the aim its head turns and tilts toward the marked patch and its eye stays on it, so you can see where it's going before it goes.
- **It pecks for real:** it flies in before the strike and drums the patch with a run of quick blows, its head a blur, chips flying with each blow and the cracks deepening. The last blow lands on the strike, as the patch gives way, so the wood never looks whole where it has already gone. Each blow is a knock in the drum you hear: a burst, fast and then slowing, as the real bird drums.
- **It says so:** its sharp "kik!" when it arrives, and when it settles back on its perch.
- **With reduced motion:** no wingbeats or bobbing, and it moves between perch and patch in one smooth glide; the eye still looks, and the blows still show, slower and fewer.
- **Cheap:** its layer is only as big as the board and its perch, and its parts are drawn from cached shapes.
- **As built** (wave 2):
  - *Flight:* bursts of wingbeats (104 ms a beat) lifting it, wings tucked in the dips (a bound about every 240 ms of flight), in a swoop; its body tipped the way it goes, upright as it climbs, near level across, never nose-down past level. A flight takes 240–540 ms by distance, shortened to fit the aim. It lands with its wings up and swept forward, leaning back, and folds them over 120 ms with a little settle.
  - *The plan* is drawn from the woodpecker's own state (the strike's tick, the patch, the last patch, how many it has struck), so every screen draws the same bird at the same moment, a friend's too. Through the aim: a hop to the new spot's perch (along the ledge or its side of the board; to the board's other side it flies straight in instead), a look of at least 260 ms, a 200 ms crouch, the flight in, the landing, then the drum. After the strike: 90 ms at the hole, the flight home, settling, and "kik!". It works on the patch's cell nearest the patch's middle (the middle itself can lie off a bent patch).
