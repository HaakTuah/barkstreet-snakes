# The owner's instructions, 4 November 2026

In the design chat (4 November 2026), the owner agreed to the design session's proposal to queue, in both games, a check
on every pull request that every pointer in the rule files leads somewhere, and one of how much each kind of session must
read (two checks done by hand at milestones until then, seen done in code by a tool outside these repositories). (written
into the workshop lane of `docs/PROGRESS.md`; the same in the other game)

In the design chat (4 November 2026), having played a little:

> You don't dash when you press the arrow keys or WASD in the direction you are moving. I think you ought to. Also, it
> feels like anything you eat should give a little stamina recharge, so you recover quicker; it can probably come after
> the one-stamina-bar rework. Right now, eating is sort of a net negative after a certain point: you never need the
> length, it just becomes a liability. If it also recharges you a bit, in line with how much food it gives you, it
> becomes a better value proposition.

Then, agreeing to the design session's answer (the steering key dashing again behind touch's guard, and food's refill
measured in 2u+) and its two questions:

> I further suggest that stamina becomes something that doesn't recharge by itself, or at least very slowly. Maybe food
> gives it, and you can reach over baseline and save up? And golden apples give a full baseline? And maybe the golden
> apple can shield you from your next bump?

(the first two written into 2aa, `docs/handoff/queue-specs.md`, and 2u+, `docs/PROGRESS.md`; the further suggestions
answered in the chat first)

In the design chat (4 November 2026), agreeing to the design session's answer (food as the main source of stamina, a
slow trickle to a floor instead of none, banking to a capped and visible overfull bar, the golden apple as a full
baseline, and the shield measured as one bump, gone after a few seconds) and its question whether to write the staged
test into 2u+:

> Two points. Maybe the cap should start at 3 times the baseline, and be worked out in testing. And I only imagined the
> golden apple's shield as absorbing one bump: if you have no space to move, it does you no good.

(written into 2u+, `docs/handoff/queue-specs.md`)

In the design chat (4 November 2026), to the design session's proposal of 2u+'s staged test:

> It will still slowly recharge. And your stamina or power bar increasing should not show as a bar above you, but rather
> as a graphical effect. Maybe snakes with an empty bar look grey, shrivelled and tired, but not too exaggerated. A
> baseline snake looks awake, with its game face on. And a snake that goes over the baseline starts looking more and
> more "on fire", but with energy, not as if it were set alight.

Then the owner agreed to the design session's answer (the look as every snake's cue, in steps that match what it can
afford; a faint ring for your own snake alone, so no price is hidden; readable without colour and unlike the golden
effects and Star's flashing; the looks file split first) and its question whether to write it into 2u and 2u+, dropping
the version with no recharge at all. (written into `docs/design/37-one-stamina.md` and 2u+, `docs/handoff/queue-specs.md`)

In the design chat (4 November 2026):

> Do we have a rule that every change that alters a feature must update its description in every document where it is
> mentioned, or ought to be? Or rather, by our rules it ought to be mentioned in only one place.

Then the owner agreed to the design session's answer (no rule said it in so many words: only the README, the lesson and
the Field guide row here, and the rulebook, seat view and design notes in the other game, were named; each reader has its
own one place, and every one of them must change together, with dated records left as they were) and its question
whether to add the rule to both games, folding the README rule into it. (written into "No description left untrue" in
`AGENTS.md` and "What blocks" in `docs/review-guidelines.md`; the same in the other game)

In the design chat (4 November 2026):

> All package versions should be updated to the latest version as a rule, unless there is a good reason not to: it
> breaks something, worse performance, a change in licence, and so on.

Then the owner agreed to the design session's answer (agreed, with four guards: the waits already set after a release, a
fixed pin moved on purpose and never a floating version, a major version in its own PR, and more reasons to hold back,
such as an abandoned project or a much bigger file; updates become upkeep that builders take without waiting for the
owner) and its question whether to write it into both games, with PeerJS 1.5.5 as the first update under it. (written
into "The newest version, unless there is a reason" in `docs/developing.md`, with a pointer in "Ideas are cheap" in
`AGENTS.md`; the same in the other game)

In the design chat (4 November 2026), to the design session's proposal that updates to what ships to players merge only
on the owner's yes:

> No: how would I have any better idea whether there is an open attack vector there? And we don't know there is none in
> the current version either.

Then, agreeing to the design session's answer (no yes from the owner, who could judge a release no better than a
builder; the automatic checks kept and their limits said plainly; and what limits the harm a bad release could do, a
content security policy in each game, queued first among the side lanes):

> But we already know who the different LLM providers are. Or can that part be an exception, since it can only pass
> formatted text anyway? I'm thinking of the future, so we don't lock ourselves in to just today's LLM providers.

The design session answered that the policy guards what a page could send out, not what providers send back, and
proposed one list of providers in one place, from which the settings screen and the policy are both made, so a new
provider is one line. (written into "The newest version, unless there is a reason" in `docs/developing.md`,
`SECURITY.md`, points 3 and 4, `tools/versions.json` and the workshop lane of `docs/PROGRESS.md`; the same in the other
game)
