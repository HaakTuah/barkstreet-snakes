## Round six B: the new moves

The owner's notes after Star turned the powers into moves you plan with: hopping between islands, flying a golden jump, flashing through a thin body (yours too), and a wrap you can see. Round six A's rules hold: every new rule has a visible cue and a lesson; every rival uses each move when it pays and sees it used against it (the owner's core premise: no ability that only people use); every rival keeps its trademark; no move may become a way to be safe.

**Every rival has a power style.** Each persona uses jumps and flashes its own way, through the same engine calls as a person:
- Sniper cuts off its target; Mute seals boxes; Gobbler races for food; Vulture flashes to beads; Ghost keeps a flash ready to escape.
- Sovereign and Colonist chase intruders off their land; Mason finishes walls; Creeper flashes in behind tails; Desperado for the thrill; Joker springs its lures; Ouroboros jumps its own coils and drops its own tail.
- Star is the addict; Wildcard is random; Capricious and Copycat borrow; Metagamer reads its rivals; Drab is the baseline.
- All of them: a ready flash is worth keeping (a reserve value); length is worth trading for room when boxed in (jump to an island, or drop the tail); keep a jumpable way out; cross the woodpecker's mark when they'll be out of it before the strike.
- *The test case:* the owner's screenshot (Gobbler folded on a strip, an island across a hollow). The rival must find the hop, the flight or the tail drop.

Its style here is the cure, not a number: it hops to the next island for food, and drops its tail when a pocket is too small. Measured: Gobbler comfortably inside 15–35%, its food share still the field's biggest.

**Part 3, the chosen numbers** (PR #183: the flashes cut, your own beads squashed, Mason's double walls; mushrooms coming back up follow in their own PR): `FLASH_THICK` 1, `DASH_THICK` 2; a failed flash costs `FAIL_LOSS` 5 and stuns `FAIL_STUN_TICKS` 6 (a crash: 3 and 4); the flash rests `FLASH_COOLDOWN_MS` 12 s (at 11 s rivals cut 10 times a round). The cut is one a snake, at its cell nearest its head among the cells the flash reaches; a layer beyond the flash's last cell is only why it can't go on (the review found a golden flash cutting, and neck-killing, a snake one cell past its reach). The rivals: a cut rival's tail is worth 0.1 apple a segment to them, up to 12, times (0.5 + aggression), and their target's twice (at 0.4 uncapped they cut 14 times a round and the beads swamped the board; at 0.15, 10 times, and the cut starved small); only the hunters and the aggressive weigh a cut off to the side; they drop their own tail only when trapped (dropped whenever boxed, 5 tails a round, and starving rose to 14% of deaths); their own beads are worth nothing to them (squashing to deny is left to people and to the pricing part). **Part 3b, the chosen numbers** (PR #195, mushrooms come back up): `CHEEK_TICKS` 9 (about a second), `SPIT_REACH` 3, `SHRINK_MAX` 5, `SPIT_WILT_MIN` 20 ticks. *Readings taken:* the spit leaves at the start of the tick its time is up, before anyone moves, from the head and the way it faces then: exactly where the ring has been (the review found the ring a cell short when it left after the move); a head whose own mouth is full makes a spit bounce off it as off a body; a spat mushroom that lands on food or a burrow rolls off by the same rule; a head over a hole or in a fire isn't swallowing: the mushroom is gone there; mushrooms in cheeks count toward the board's quota, so the log holds no more poison than before. *The rivals:* everyone aims one in the cheek (the last moves before the spit), closes in on where it could hit a head before that, and keeps its head out of any ring due within its search; eating one on purpose is weighed only at a mushroom within 4 of its head with a rival's head close and a line to spit along, as a shot worth `w.spit` × 0.35 × half a segment for each it would take off (Joker 4 "for the laugh", Sniper 2.5 "for the head shot", Ghost 0.2, Drab and those built on Drab 0.05, the rest 0.5; Gobbler's hatred caps it); without a shot a mushroom costs what it always did. *Measured* on fresh seeds 71 and 73 (1,200 paired rounds, head to head, against the same AI that never aims nor eats one on purpose, both dodging): the spitters win 25.2% to 24.6% (+0.6 points, interval −2.5 to +3.7), live as long (156 s), eat as much, kill as often, die of the same causes; every trademark the same or within noise (Vulture's beads 31.3% to 33.4%, within noise). **A design finding:** between rivals a spit hardly ever lands (1 to 2% of spits hit a head, about the same when nobody dodges, and as rare with a cheek of 3 ticks): the rivals keep their heads apart, so a head is seldom 3 cells in line from a spitter, and a ring a second ahead is easy to step out of. It's a skill shot for people, and the rivals dodge theirs. The owner's answer (30 October 2026): the spit becomes a shot along the line, part 3b+ (the question and answer: `docs/archive/progress-done.md`, "For the owner, answered").

**Part 4, the chosen numbers** (PR #198: the golden flight, leap now, bumps in the air, and 2r's doomed islands): `GOLD_FLY` 5 (a held golden jump flies up to 5 cells, one turn planned, a person's turn a tick at a time), `LEAP_WINDOW` 5, leap now down on the fourth cell (`LEAP_REACH` + 1) or the farthest free short of it.
- **The rivals:**
  - They plan a flight at take-off and again every tick in the air: the straight landings and one turn, each landing only where no rival's head can get there first.
  - A landing that shuts a nearby rival in is worth apples: the share of the room that rival reaches in 10 moves my landed body takes away (4 apples for all of it), and the moves it adds to its nearest food (2 apples at 12 moves or more). Times (0.5 + aggression, +0.5 for a hunter), its victim twice, half for a rival holding a golden jump (`shutIn`). The owner: "someone really skillful can time cutting someone else off midair".
  - Leap now only out of a trap, when every crawl crashes. Used over open ground, or over a coming head-on, it cost them.
  - A held golden dash is kept for a trap, or for a cut that puts a rival out. Star spends them as they come (`dashSpend`): its trademark.
  - 2r: no hop onto an island too small to live on, unless it can hop off again in time (`doomed`).
- **Measured,** head to head on fresh seeds, each move taken from the same AI by itself:
  - *The golden dash, kept* (seeds 659/661, 1,200 paired rounds): place 2.46 to 2.54 and time alive 156 s to 155 s (both decided); win 25.8% to 24.0% (not decided).
  - *The flight* (seeds 641/643, 1,200): 24.7% to 25.1%, even (interval −2.9 to +3.6).
  - *Leap now* (seeds 647/653, 800): 25.0% to 24.8%, decided the same.
  - Every trademark the same or within noise in all three.
  - Per use (`tools/dev/fork-moves.js --move fly --rest`, the round forked at each flight and played on with and without it): +0.09 ± 0.07 place a flight (seeds 701–704, n 406), −0.04 before `shutIn`.
  - Eleven other plans were measured the same way and dropped: keeping golden charges for flights, a ply of search after landing, charging contested landings, and Fable's design (an honest landing price, committing in the air, purpose flights with seals priced as kills).
- **A design finding** (issue #363): a snake flies past the plain hop only about 0.4 times a round, so +0.09 a flight is under a win point a round, inside the noise. Leap now out of a trap is rarer still. Both pay when used, and their worth is a person's timing.
