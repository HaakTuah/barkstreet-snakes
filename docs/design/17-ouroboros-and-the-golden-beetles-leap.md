## Ouroboros, and the golden beetle's leap

**Ouroboros** (the snake that eats its own tail) is the 17th rival, and the first that looks like a real snake: dark gold with a python's dark diamonds down its back (a pale fleck in each), a wedge-shaped head with a diamond on the crown, amber eyes with black slits, nostrils. Its tongue gag is its fangs (only people get a forked tongue), and when its own tail is right in front of it, it opens wide and bites it: the symbol itself, for a moment. Its remains are gold beads with a dark diamond. **How it plays:** a new `coil` weight rewards keeping its head close behind its own tail once it's 10 or longer, so it moves as a ring (head within 3 cells of its tail about two thirds of the time when long); food pulls it along, so the ring rolls over food, and anything caught inside is boxed in. It's distinct from Mute (who boxes in one chosen victim) and Sovereign (who guards land). A two-headed, head-on-tail look was considered and turned down: in a game where which end is the head decides everything, a decoy head would mislead you. **Tuned in the simulator:** at first it won 32-34% (the ring is a strong, safe shape; a stronger coil made it stronger still, 39%), so it got a gentler coil (weight 20 from length 10), less caution and less appetite: over 1600 seeded rounds it wins 31%, level with Mason at the top of a field running 16% to 31%. Its lines are cyclical ("All things return.", "Full circle, Green.", "…and begin again.").

**Golden beetles and the leap.** About one beetle in three (`GOLD_BEETLE_CHANCE`; two in five since round four, one in six since round six A, with beetles out more often) comes out golden: a gold shell rimmed in emerald, a glow and glints. Catch one and you get `LEAP_CHARGES` (2) leaps (`LEAP_MAX` 3 held), shown as a Honey pip for each on a short arc behind your head (it was a golden ring round it; see the third pass). **To leap, press the way you're already going** (on a phone, swipe forward; for a friend online, it's the same message as a turn; superseded by round four: Space or a tap jumps, golden leaps first, and online it's a message of its own): if there's a body or a hole right in front and a free cell beyond, you hop over it, moving two cells in one tick, your body arching over what's in between (still joined up, so nothing else changes); the head arcs up through the air, drawn on top until the arch has run off your tail. The leap waits `LEAP_WINDOW` (3) ticks for something to hop and isn't spent on open ground (so pressing early or mashing the key wastes nothing), and never goes off the board or onto a body. **The rivals leap too**, without asking: whenever they'd otherwise crash. It pairs with the crash rules: a leap is the way out of a box. One power, not a set: warp and wrap-around were considered and left out (they'd make the edges meaningless and muddle the woodpecker's work); if the leap feels good, another could follow. (It did: see Powers. Everyone leaps now, the golden beetle's leap is the long one, and the rivals ask for theirs the way a person does.) About 0.8 golden beetles come out a round (1.6 since round four). The narrator announces them ("A golden beetle! Catch it, and you can leap over things."), explains the leap when you catch one, and a springy boing goes with every leap. Online it's protocol 5.
