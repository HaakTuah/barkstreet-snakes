## Sound

**Borders sound like borders** (round four). When your head crosses into a rival's land (Sovereign's realm or one of Colonist's colonies), a muffled drum knocks twice and a fife answers with a short call, panned to where you are. It plays once per crossing, at most every 0.8 s. (With the force fields it was a low electric hum: two detuned saws, filtered, with a rising shimmer and a fizz of noise.)

**Round four's other cues.** A plain flash is a short whoosh of air, lighter than a golden flash's zip, and your flash coming back from its rest is a small ember spark (the jump's is a wooden tick). The last apples starting to fall get soft notes falling down the minor key over a low note.

**Round six A's cues.** A cornered beetle digging in is a quick scrabbling bore: legs scratching, a rasp sinking into the wood, a low grind a phone can hear, a puff of sawdust. The woodpecker knocking food aside is a hollow tok and a whoosh as it flies, with a soft bump where it lands (as it's drawn landing), at half volume. Both are world cues, their gaps (300 and 400 ms) under the soonest each can come again, so beetles digging in together, or one strike's pieces, are one sound. A dig is heard as yours when your head cornered the beetle, else as a rival's, about 6 dB down; since the critique a rival's keeps its legs' scratches past the dulling, at its own softer volume, so it's heard over the sudden-death music too (it lifts it 9.9 dB at 3–6 kHz, from 5.9). A beetle going down a burrow or coming up keeps its quiet scrabble, called `scrabble` now (it was `dig`). The burrow a dig leaves makes no sound of its own (the bore said it), and a burrow closing over is a muffled crumble, sawdust trickling in with a fleck ticking in high over each grain, and a hushed pat of moss. Since the critique it's 12 dB louder than it was (it was softer than the scrabble, and the music hid it): in sudden death it now lifts the music 8.8 dB at 3–6 kHz and 7.8 above 6 kHz, where it lifted nothing. Rendered offline as the game plays them, at their loudest 50 ms: the dig −32.7 dBFS (a rival's −38.4), about 8 dB under an apple (−24.7) and 9 over a beetle's skitter (−41.6); the knock −25.2, about an apple's; the crumble −40.3, about a beetle's skitter, 9 dB over the scrabble (−49.8; until the critique the crumble was −52.9, 3 dB under it). (*How they're measured:* in an `OfflineAudioContext` a cue that starts at the render's very start comes out quieter than the same cue 0.2 s later, by up to 10 dB for a sharp attack: an apple −34.3 against −24.7, the dig −34.0 against −32.7. Round six A's first numbers were rendered that way, which under-read the apple most, so the dig seemed level with it. These are rendered after a silent lead-in, with the game's own options: the knock at half volume, a rival's flagged.) (See "Less fruit, more beetles" and "The woodpecker plays fair" under round six A; the choke and the cough are under "Choking on your own tail".)

**The woodpecker's drum is its blows** (round six A). The game starts the drum from the aim's progress, on the drawing's clock, so each knock is a blow you see and its last knock lands on the strike, with the crash (before, the drum started at the strike and rang on 0.68 s after it); watched faster, it plays faster, and started late, it leaves out the knocks already past. The drum is 520 ms (it was 676), and its first three strikes have 14 knocks, then 12, 10, 8 and 6. Settling back on its perch after a strike, the bird says one softer "kik!" (its three-kik call stays for its arrival). See "A woodpecker you can believe".

**The second sound pass: tension you can feel.** Every cue can come from where it happens (a stereo pan from the board's x). Apples eaten in quick succession climb a semitone each (up to seven), so a streak sings. Taking a rival out lands a knockout punch (a low thump, a crack, two bright notes); any other death is a far-off thud. A near miss (your head next to a rival's) whooshes past. First blood gets a dark minor stab; the last two, and the countdown's last beat, a riser. The woodpecker calls "kik-kik-kik" with a beat of wings, ticks tensely as it takes aim, and drums a burst of knocks that slows like a real one, as the chunk crunches out. Winter has glassy bells; a burning leaf, a whoosh and a crackle. Your death lands with a soft impact before its sigh. **The music feels the danger:** each tick the game measures how close a rival's head is to yours and how much room is ahead; past a quarter, a heartbeat (lub-dub on the beat) comes in, the bass drives on the off-beats, and past six tenths a dissonant minor second creeps into the tune. The arrangements are fuller: a soft, slightly detuned pad holds each bar's chord, bells answer the tune mid-round, sudden death adds low toms, and the duel races at 124 bpm with hats on every sixteenth. **The weather has its own sound**, looping beds of filtered noise on the music bus: rain hisses, wind swells and falls, a storm rumbles under a little rain, winter is a high, airy hush; a new one fades in as the old fades out.

Every sound is synthesised live with the Web Audio API, through one AudioContext, created on the first key or tap (browsers require a gesture). There are no audio files, so it works offline with nothing to download.

- **Cues** are short and round: a marimba pluck for an apple (nudged in pitch each time, so a run of apples doesn't grate), a rising sparkle for gold, a low "nom" for meat, a wobbling "boing" for a mushroom, a far-off thud when a rival dies, a two-note sigh for your death, a fanfare for a new best and a bouncier phrase for a win, wood-block countdown beats and a "go", the woodpecker's knock-knock and pecks, and a tummy gurgle when you start starving.
- **Mixing:** everything goes through a compressor so four snakes eating at once can't clip. At most 6 cues sound at once (death, best and win always get through), and each cue has a minimum gap so it can't machine-gun.
- **Music** is scheduled note by note on the audio clock, a quarter second ahead, from the game loop, and switches arrangement at bar lines so changes land on the beat. It has its own bus and its own switch (N), separate from all sound (M). Both are remembered.


**Rivals' chomps** (2d, Haakon, 29 October 2026). Every snake's meal is heard now, not only yours: a rival's plays the same cue as yours for that food (apple, golden apple, bead, mushroom, beetle, golden leaf), dulled like any rival's cue and softer still (`RIVAL_CHOMP` 0.6 of a rival's leap or dash, so 0.3 of your own chomp, about 10 dB down), panned to where it ate, never climbing the streak's scale (that stays yours). Never a pile-up: one rival's chomp at a time, at least `CHOMP_GAP` (180 ms, two of the fastest ticks) apart whoever ate, and none for `CHOMP_YIELD` (350 ms, about as long as your longest chomp rings) after yours; the round asks for yours first in a tick, so four snakes eating together are your chomp alone. Yours never waits on theirs. A golden mushroom keeps its rival wrap (its own 1.5 s gap, at a rival's usual level). Online, each player's game hears every snake's eat from the events it already gets, so no protocol change.
