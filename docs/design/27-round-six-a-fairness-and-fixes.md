## Round six A: fairness and fixes

After Star, the owner played and sent a long run of notes. They became a plan of three PRs, built one after another:
- **A** (this one): fairness and fixes;
- **B**: the new moves and the rivals' power styles;
- **C**: weather, fire, polish, a gentler start and a last optimization pass.

Three rules from the owner hold for all of them:
- **Every new rule has a visible cue and a lesson** (a one-time banner, and a row in the Field guide). The owner called it a "very important point": "Add that!"
- **Every rival accounts for every change** (the owner: "make sure every rival account for every gameplay change you make!"). It uses the new rule when it pays and sees it used against it, through the same engine calls as a person.
- **Every rival keeps its trademark.** Mason's straight lines (the owner: "Keep watch!"), Ouroboros's coils, Creeper's tail-riding and the rest are measured before and after.

**Choking on your own tail** (the owner: "You can always turn on a dime, so feels like you almost always get there first. … what if you choke on your own bones, and spit them back out?"). Your own beads (the tail a crash knocks off, or a cut takes) can't be eaten back any more:
- **The choke.** Your head gags on one and spits it aside; it stays food for anyone else. You lose nothing, but you don't get your crash back.
  - *Why:* you can turn on a dime, so you were nearly always first to your own beads; 24% of bead meals were the eater's own. A crash cost almost nothing, and the planned "drop your tail" move would have cost nothing either.
- **The cough** (the owner's addition: "maybe it makes so you can't open your mouth and gulp down other food for a short moment"). After choking, you cough for about a second and can't eat. It never chains: more of your own beads met while coughing are just spat aside.
- **Nudging** (the owner asked: "If the food stays put, then it stays under you as you pass over it?"). Food your coughing head reaches is nudged aside, out in the open, where a rival can take it: a cell, or further on over food in the way. (Left under you, it would be hidden from everyone and waiting for you.)
- **Where it rolls is a rule, never chance** (the owner: "If you randomly nudge it to the opposite side of a opponent, they will feel cheated. Ideally it is rules based somehow."; and, once it was: "Then rivals can predict where things will end up. Nice!"):
  1. turning into it, it's knocked to the inside of the turn, beside your neck; to the outside only if the inside is taken;
  2. going straight, it rolls to the side with more open log;
  3. a tie goes toward the middle;
  4. if that side is taken, it goes to the other; if both are, it rolls on the way it would have gone first, over food or a burrow, to the first free cell within 4, and failing that the other way. It stays under your head only when both ways are shut by the edge, a hole, fire or a body.

  A spat bead goes by the same rule. Rivals predict it, and so can you.

**Rivals see food as it is.** Rivals see other snakes a quarter of a second late, like a person's reaction, and DESIGN only ever promised that. By accident they saw food that late too. It didn't matter for apples, which don't move. For beetles it did: they chased where a beetle *was*, wiggled behind it and lost it ("self-sabotage", the owner said). Now:
- food is seen as it is now;
- the delay stays for snakes;
- a rival aims where a fleeing beetle is heading, from the way it faces and its pace, which anyone can see: straight on, then away from the nearest head toward a burrow nearer than `BEETLE_HOME_REACH` (7), and diving in. Since wave 2, a cornered one (a head within `BEETLE_DIG_FEAR` and no burrow in reach) digs in where it is and stays put until it's gone. Every move the search tries moves every beetle that's out (two at once since wave 2), and from where each search ends each chase is played out;
- catching sooner counts twice what a step closer to an apple does (`BUG_HURRY`), so a catch in hand isn't dropped for an errand;
- rivals see every burrow on the board, as a person does (`view.burrows`, since the rivals' pass in wave 2). Until then they learned the burrows by watching beetles come out of them: they missed a burrow a dig left until a beetle used it, and remembered ones that had closed over (about 2.5 close a round), so they expected scared beetles to dive into burrows that were gone.

**The woodpecker plays fair** (the owner saw Mute shut in by a mark beside it, with no way to plan for it, and said the bird "maybe refuses to peck where there is apples and mushrooms. Makes for some rather oddly shaped holes, with 1 tile islands."). A study played each idea alone and together, 300 seeded rounds to screen and 600 on two seeds to confirm. What's kept:
- **It won't shut you in if it can help it.** It won't pick a patch that leaves any snake less room than its length (room judged with the real timings, tails leaving), while it has another to pick. (Called "it never shuts you in" at first, and said so in the lesson and the Field guide; with nowhere else to aim it still may, 0.08 marks a round, so the verify pass softened the words.)

- **The log stays one piece** (2s, built 1 November 2026; it grows "No crumbs" to any size. Haakon, 31 October 2026: "we want to avoid the islands stuff. it becomes too much rng, and isn't very exciting", and "No outline should be banned from falling, but ideally, it never takes out half the trunk."). The log is the biggest piece of bark once a patch is out; any other piece the patch cuts loose goes into the mark from the aim (`w.fall`), shaded dark with pale streaks sliding down it, and falls with the strike: it tips away into the pit with a creak and a far-off thud, food and beetles on it gone with it, a head still on it down with it ("Down the hole!"; a body lying over it bridges the new hole, as over any patch). - **It flies like one:** a few quick wingbeats, then wings tucked in a dip, then beats again (the bounding flight that tells a woodpecker from other birds), its body tipping with the swoop. It lands with a flare of the wings and a little settle.
- **Perched, it's alive:** it clings upright, braced on its stiff tail as woodpeckers do, and shifts its grip now and then. It bobs a little, and its head moves the way birds' heads move: quick jerks, then still.
- **It looks:** its eye looks at things, as the snakes' eyes do. Resting, it glances at whatever moves nearby, now and then at you, and blinks. Through the aim its head turns and tilts toward the marked patch and its eye stays on it, so you can see where it's going before it goes.
- **It pecks for real:** it flies in before the strike and drums the patch with a run of quick blows, its head a blur, chips flying with each blow and the cracks deepening.
